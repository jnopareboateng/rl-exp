drugs = drugs with a value; rows = prompt rows used; skip = rows with fewer than 8 samples (unusable for the @8 metrics); deg = every unit has the same value, so the interval has zero
  width and understates the uncertainty (a rate of 0 over n drugs is only bounded near 3/n); * = bootstrap interval excludes 0 (descriptive).
exact@8 and core@8 are oracle coverage: they need the answer to pick the sample, never a deployable result.
fv_tanimoto is the confirmatory endpoint: the Tanimoto of the first valid finished answer among the first 8 samples of a row (0 if none). Its cost is stated, not matched:
  read it with fv_samples (draws per answer) and fv_tokens (generated tokens up to the first valid answer; prompt_tokens adds the prompt): an arm that invalidates its weak answers can raise it by paying in draws.
  every other metric is a diagnostic. tanimoto_valid conditions on a valid finished answer: read it beside valid_closed.
lift = Tanimoto of the answer to its own drug's reference minus its mean Tanimoto to the other drugs' references of the split (0 for an invalid, unclosed or length-terminated answer); fv_lift is the lift of the first valid answer;
  they measure the association between the drug and everything the policy does. A constant answer has lift 0 over a complete panel only if completion does not depend on the drug
  (a drug-dependent finish rate, or a cue such as prompt length, can raise lift without the answer using the profile); fv_lift of a constant is 0 when every row has a valid answer.
Intervals and p-values are per metric and not adjusted for multiplicity: only fv_tanimoto, the endpoint declared in docs/contracts.md, is confirmatory,
  and for it the sign-flip p-value, not the bootstrap interval, is the decision statistic.
Sign-flip test assumptions: the components (cluster_id groups, or single drugs when rows carry no cluster_id) are the exchangeable units and are independent;
  flipping the sign of each component's summed A - B difference is valid under a null in which those sums are symmetric about zero (a zero mean alone is not enough);
  the p-value holds only under those assumptions: it is not assumption-free.
