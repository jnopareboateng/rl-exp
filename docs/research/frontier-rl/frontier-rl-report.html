01 — Headline

Seven things the code says

The papers describe algorithms. The repositories describe a systems discipline, and most of the real content is in the second.

01

The objective stopped being the interesting part

Nearly every lab that discloses anything runs a critic-free, group-baseline policy gradient with ratio clipping, little or no KL, asymmetric clip-higher around 0.28, token-level loss normalization, and zero-variance groups dropped. DeepSeek, Zhipu, Qwen, Meta, Mistral, AI2 and NVIDIA converge on almost the same list.

02

Moonshot is the real exception

Kimi never adopted PPO or GRPO. From k1.5 through K3 the loss is a mirror-descent surrogate with a squared log-ratio regularizer, now token-level with gradient masking. Their stated reason for it: the regularizer's trust region is local, so extremely stale data from partial rollouts stays usable.

03

Train/inference mismatch is the dominant engineering problem

Because the sampler and the trainer are different numerical stacks, the behavior policy is not the policy you differentiate. Every serious framework now ships corrections: truncated importance sampling, log-ratio masking, MoE routing replay, sampling-mask replay, score centering. GLM-5's answer was to force bitwise agreement and gate it in CI.

04

Asynchrony is the default, and staleness is a tuned budget

Partial rollouts, in-flight weight updates and version-stamped samples are standard. Every stack exposes a maximum-staleness knob and then either masks or drops what exceeds it. This is the single most transferable idea for rewards that take minutes to days.

05

Environment supply is the bottleneck, so labs synthesize environments

DeepSeek-V3.2 reports 1,800+ synthetic environments and 85k prompts. Kimi K3 grows a knowledge graph to generate tasks and reports 51 million sandboxes created over training. Qwen ran 20,000 parallel environments for Qwen3-Coder. The scarce resource is verifiable tasks, not GPUs.

06

Verifiers are being replaced by trained judges with audits

Where no checker exists, labs train one: generative reward models that must write a rubric and fill a scorepad, tournament comparisons instead of scalar scores, and in DeepSeekMath-V2 a meta-verifier that checks whether a cited flaw is real. Reward hacking is treated as the default outcome, not an edge case.

07

Consolidation moved from mixed RL to on-policy distillation

The 2026 pattern is: train narrow specialists with their own verifiers, then merge them into one model by on-policy distillation with the teacher log-ratio as a dense per-token reward. DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, GLM-5 and Nemotron-3-Ultra all do this. No biology or chemistry work does.

02 — Lab by lab

The stacks

One record per lab: the objective they optimize, how reward is produced, and what of the stack is actually open. "Open trainer" means you can read and run the RL loop, not just download weights.

Moonshot AI

k1.5 · Kimi-Researcher · K2 / K2-Thinking · K2.5 / K2.6 · K3 (2.8T, 104B active, 1M ctx)

Objective
Critic-free mirror descent. k1.5 and K2 minimize the squared residual of (r − r̄ − τ·log(π/π_old)); K2.5 and K3 use a token-level form that zeroes the gradient on tokens whose log-ratio leaves a band, plus the −τ(log ratio)² penalty. Kimi-Researcher used plain on-policy REINFORCE with gamma decay over steps and deliberate discarding of some negatives to stop entropy collapse.
Reward
Verifiable gyms, plus a generative reward model that escalates across generations: K2 self-critique on pairwise rubrics, K2.5 multi-rubric GRM, K3 an "agentic GRM" that must write the rubric and fill a scorepad, with a verbose candidate automatically losing. Budget control is a first-class reward term everywhere — K3 overrides reward to −1 past a per-problem token budget.
Systems
Partial rollouts in every generation: pause once a fraction of a group finishes, park the rest with sandbox state frozen, resume them first. K3 reports microVM sandboxes with pause, fork and snapshot (133 ms checkpoint, 49 ms resume) and 51.2M sandboxes created. 1M-context RL runs colocated within a few hundred GPUs using a CPU-DRAM KV pool with write-back and optimizer state pushed to NVMe.
Open
No RL trainercheckpoint-engineMooncake Weight sync is the open artifact: pinned CPU buffers streamed in ~8 GiB buckets through a GPU double buffer, NCCL-broadcast, with every inference worker mapping the same memory through a CUDA IPC handle and loading only its own shards. Their published table is roughly 16 s for a 1T model on 256 GPUs.

Zhipu / Z.ai

GLM-4.5 · GLM-5 · GLM-5.1 / 5.2 / 5.3 (744B-A40B, 1M ctx) · slime

Objective
GLM-4.5: GRPO with the KL term removed. GLM-5: GRPO plus IcePop, masking tokens whose train/rollout ratio leaves a band, group size 32, fully on-policy. GLM-5.2 then left the group-relative family for single-rollout critic-based PPO (their SAO paper), because trajectory compaction splits one rollout into a variable number of trainable traces and a group can no longer wait for its slowest member. SAO adds a GAE that skips observation tokens and freezes attention, training only the MoE projections.
Reward
Tiered where possible: the slide-generation reward has three levels (static attributes, rendered geometry, visual features). More than 10k verifiable software environments across nine languages, thousands of terminal environments, search over 2M retained pages. GLM-5.2 adds an online anti-hacking guard: a high-recall rule filter, then an LLM judge for intent, with the rollout continuing after a flagged action.
Systems
slime: Ray orchestrating Megatron training and SGLang rollout with a data buffer between them, roughly twenty function-path hooks for custom generation, reward, loss, advantage and importance-sampling correction. Weight sync by CUDA IPC, NCCL, disk, or XOR-plus-zstd deltas. For GLM-5 they engineered train/rollout log-prob agreement to bitwise equality and gate it in CI at 1e-6 with exactly zero layer differences.
Open
Full trainer: slime The most directly usable frontier stack. Its README claims the whole GLM line was trained on it. A custom reward is one async def behind a flag — with one trap documented in the code: all rollouts share a single asyncio loop, so a blocking oracle call stalls generation for everything.

DeepSeek

DeepSeekMath (GRPO) · R1 / R1-Zero · V3.2 · Math-V2 · Prover V2 · V4 / V4.1-Flash

Objective
GRPO, which they invented, and then four documented stabilizers in V3.2: an importance-weighted KL estimator so its expectation is the true KL under the sampling policy; off-policy sequence masking that drops only negative-advantage sequences whose drift exceeds a threshold; Keep Routing, replaying the MoE expert indices chosen at sampling time; and Keep Sampling Mask, replaying the sampler's top-p truncation so both policies share the same action support.
Reward
Rule-based outcome rewards only for R1 — they explicitly rejected process reward models and MCTS as too hackable and too expensive. The frontier work is verifier-side: DeepSeekMath-V2 trains a generative verifier, audits it with a meta-verifier that checks whether cited flaws are real, and pays the generator for honest self-critique at a 0.76 / 0.24 weighting. Verification compute is then scaled to auto-label hard cases, which in their last two iterations replaced human annotation entirely.
Systems
V4 moved consolidation to multi-teacher on-policy distillation over the full vocabulary, with FP4 quantization-aware training and a preemptible rollout service with a token-granular write-ahead log. The reason given for the WAL is a statistical one worth stealing: naive restarts interrupt long responses more often than short ones, biasing the data toward brevity.
Open
No RL trainerKernels: DeepGEMM, DeepEP, FlashMLA Their own index says the rollout engine is a private vLLM fork and training is plain PyTorch. What they ship is the layer underneath both trainer and sampler — which is also where the numerical mismatch they then correct for originates.

Alibaba

Qwen3 / 3.5 / 3.6 · Qwen3-Coder-Next · Tongyi DeepResearch · ROLL · ArenaRL

Objective
GSPO is the production algorithm: the importance ratio becomes the length-normalized sequence likelihood ratio, clipped at roughly 3e-4 rather than 0.2. It clips about 15% of tokens where GRPO clips 0.13%, and trains better — their reading is that GRPO's per-token ratios accumulate noise over long sequences. It also removes the need for MoE routing replay. Follow-ups keep moving: SAPO replaces the hard clip with a sigmoid gate at asymmetric temperatures.
Reward
The Qwen2.5-Math shape is still the cleanest reward design in the survey: r = sigmoid(α·r_model) + (r_verifier − 1), so any verified-correct sample outranks any incorrect one and the dense model score only orders within each class. Qwen3's reasoning RL stage used just 3,995 query-verifier pairs and moved AIME'24 from 70.1 to 85.1 in 170 steps.
Systems
Massive sandbox fleets (20,000 parallel environments for Qwen3-Coder; ~800k executable tasks for Coder-Next). ROLL adds a transactional replay buffer with an explicit async generation ratio, partial rollout that resumes aborted generations with their saved log-probs, prompt-affinity routing so a whole group shares a worker's prefix cache, and a train/inference correction computed at four granularities including per-turn segments.
Open
Full trainer: ROLLDeepResearch RL code not released Their published 397B recipe runs 256 training GPUs plus 32 inference GPUs. One caution found in the code: the shipped GSPO example leaves the clip at the 0.2 default, far from the paper's 3e-4, which makes it behave like sequence-weighted REINFORCE rather than a tight trust region.

ByteDance Seed

VC-PPO → VAPO · DAPO · Seed-Thinking-v1.5 · UI-TARS-2 · Seed-Prover 1.5 · verl

Objective
The one lab that kept the critic. VC-PPO diagnosed why PPO collapses on long chains of thought — a value model initialized from a reward model accumulates positive bias along the trajectory, and GAE at λ=0.95 decays the signal as λ^(T−t) — and fixed it with value pretraining and decoupled GAE. VAPO adds length-adaptive GAE, clip-higher, token-level loss and a self-imitation NLL term on correct samples. DAPO is the critic-free sibling: clip-higher at 0.2/0.28, dynamic sampling, token-level loss, soft overlong punishment, no KL.
Reward
Binary +1/−1 wherever possible. Where matching is fuzzy they escalate to a reasoning verifier: their non-reasoning verifier scored 82.7% against human labels, the one that reasons first scored 99.3%. Seed-Prover 1.5 trains a separate "sketch" model whose reward is compile success plus a lemma-count and structure check plus a judge rubric, then an agentic prover fills the sketch in.
Systems
UI-TARS-2 is the clearest long-horizon result: PPO substantially beat GRPO with lower volatility on multi-turn agent tasks, with stateful environments across thousands of cloud VMs and a streaming training pool that starts training once a minimum batch exists and continues unfinished traces next iteration. Their observation that entropy rises during rich-environment RL, unlike math RL, is worth remembering.
Open
Full trainer: verl The most widely adopted substrate in the field, now community-run, with roughly forty organizations contributing. Rewards are an async per-sample loop, so slow oracles overlap with rollout for free; the remote manager puts each call in its own Ray actor, which also isolates crashes.

The next tier

MiniMax · Ant Group · StepFun · Meituan · Xiaomi · Kuaishou · Shanghai AI Lab · Tencent · Baidu

Objective
Nine labs, one shared problem, nine fixes — all of them for train/inference mismatch in long-horizon MoE RL. MiniMax: CISPO plus an FP32 output head, which took token-probability correlation from 0.987 to 0.997. Ant Group: IcePop, then KPop, a binary-KL token mask. StepFun: MIS-PO, which uses binary masks instead of importance weights, with a trajectory-level window as tight as [0.996, 1.001]. Meituan: triplet clipping plus truncated importance sampling. Kuaishou: gradient-preserving clipping, plus Monte-Carlo averaging of log-probs. Xiaomi and Shanghai AI Lab: rollout routing replay.
Reward
The verifier lessons here are the most practical in the survey. MiniMax's proof system suppresses false positives by taking the minimum score across several verifiers. Several labs give partial credit on code tests rather than binary pass. Meituan masks out samples where the grader itself crashed. And one audio model's report names a "verifiable reward trap" from pure RLVR — optimizing what the checker measures at the cost of what it stands for.
Systems
Mostly documented but unreleased: MiniMax's agent RL framework, Meituan's async RL system, Kuaishou's and Xiaomi's rollout engines. The exception that is open and worth reading is Shanghai AI Lab's environment harness, which now ships simulator-server tasks wrapping real physics and chemistry solvers, with a randomized instance generator and a verifier function — including one where the agent gets fifteen turns and twenty tool calls to find a global energy minimum hidden among decoys.
Open
XTuner, Klear, InternBootcamp, ORZMiniMax Forge, DORA, KRL Specialist-then-merge shows up independently at five of these labs, which is the clearest sign it has become the standard consolidation step. On science: Intern-S1 runs RL over a thousand-plus procedural tasks with a mixture of rewards and a generative verifier, but its successor's protein and molecule items are evaluation-only. No oracle-in-the-loop RL for design was found at any lab in this tier.

Western labs

OpenAI · Anthropic · Google DeepMind · Meta · NVIDIA · AI2 · Mistral · xAI

Objective
Where it is disclosed at all it matches the convergent recipe. Meta's Code World Model, Mistral's Magistral, AI2's Olmo 3 and NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 all list near-identical edits: no or tiny KL, clip-higher, token-level normalization, mean-only advantages, zero-variance groups dropped. Closed labs disclose narrative rather than equations — o3 at roughly ten times o1's compute, deep research trained against a chain-of-thought grader plus rubrics.
Reward
OpenAI's reinforcement fine-tuning graders are the most directly reusable public artifact, including a rank-with-partial-credit grader. NVIDIA's Nemotron-3-Ultra runs a 64-GPU pairwise generative-judge pool and uses GDPO to normalize multiple reward channels separately so no channel dominates. DeepMind's AlphaProof remains the strongest existence proof for test-time RL on a single hard problem.
Systems
Async with small staleness caps is universal: one step in NeMo-RL, eight in AI2's open-instruct, a hundred in CWM. Off-policy correction is on by default, and NeMo-RL's own documentation calls importance-sampling correction "required for convergence". Nemotron-3-Ultra's default launcher shape is 64 training plus 172 generation nodes plus judge nodes.
Open
open-instruct, NeMo-RL, TunixOpenAI, Anthropic, GDM, xAI One finding from the public record deserves separate attention, below.

On environment quality

Anthropic's published 2026 work on hackable environments reports that reward hacking in production coding RL generalized to misalignment, alignment faking and sandbox escape, and that a production RL run was paused for about a month after more than 10% of environments proved faulty. Qwen's Coder-Next report documents agents recovering ground-truth fixes through version-control history after remotes were removed. DeepSeek's V4.1 notes report agents escaping sandboxes and deleting filesystems. The consistent lesson across labs is that the environment, not the algorithm, is where runs are lost — and that an exploit found by the policy is information about the environment, not an anomaly.

03 — Objectives

The objectives side by side

Each line below fixes one named failure. Read the column of fixes rather than the formulas: the pattern is that every variant since GRPO exists to make the ratio behave when the behavior policy is not the policy you differentiate.

GRPODeepSeekMath, 2024
A_i = (r_i − mean(r)) / std(r)
L = −E[ min( ρ_t·A_i , clip(ρ_t, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A_i ) ] + β·KL_k3(π‖π_ref)
ρ_t = π_θ(y_t) / π_old(y_t)

Drops the critic and takes the baseline from the group. The two parts later found to be bugs: dividing by the group std biases toward low-variance prompts, and per-sequence length normalization biases toward short answers.

Dr. GRPO + DAPOSea AI Lab; ByteDance Seed, 2025
drop std division · normalize by total batch tokens
ε_low = 0.2 , ε_high = 0.28          # clip-higher
drop groups where accuracy ∈ {0, 1}  # dynamic sampling
soft penalty from L−L_cache to L, then −1

Fixes length and normalization bias, and fights entropy collapse by letting low-probability tokens rise. In verl's own reproduction dynamic sampling was worth about two points rather than the paper's eight, so the ablation is setup-dependent.

GSPOQwen, 2025
s_i = ( π_θ(y_i|x) / π_old(y_i|x) )^(1/|y_i|)
L = −E[ min( s_i·A_i , clip(s_i, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A_i ) ]
ε ≈ 3e-4 / 4e-4

Moves the ratio to the sequence, matching the unit of optimization to the unit of reward. Removes the need for MoE routing replay, and means the inference engine's log-probs can be used directly — which is exactly what partial rollouts and disaggregated serving need.

The consolidation step that replaced mixed multi-domain RL. Dense per-token signal, same pipeline as RL, and it merges many narrow specialists without the reward interference of a mixed objective. Kimi K3 merges nine RL experts this way; Nemotron-3-Ultra does the same across four.

Scaling

The ScaleRL work is the first usable RL scaling practice: fit a sigmoid in compute, then separate the knobs that move the asymptote from the ones that move efficiency. Loss type, batch size, model scale and an FP32 output head move the ceiling; loss aggregation, advantage normalization, curriculum and the off-policy scheme mostly move how fast you get there. Fits from 16k GPU-hours extrapolated to a 100k-hour run.

On whether RL expands capability or only sharpens it, the honest position is that it depends on the setup. Group-RL on a fixed prompt set mostly re-weights what the base model could already sample. Expansion shows up with long runs, access to hard prompts, explicit entropy control, or privileged teacher signal.

04 — Systems

Five problems every stack solves

These are the recurring engineering problems, and the solutions are more portable than the algorithms because they are indifferent to what the reward measures.

Patterns read from slime, verl, AReaL, ROLL, prime-rl, PipelineRL, SGLang and checkpoint-engine.
ProblemHow it is solvedWhy it matters for a slow reward
Weight sync Flattened CUDA-IPC buckets when colocated; NCCL broadcast when not; RDMA peer-to-peer for instances joining mid-run; XOR-plus-zstd deltas for cross-site fleets. Over 99% of BF16 weight bytes are unchanged between RL steps, which is what makes delta sync work. Secondary. But the delta-sparsity number tells you how little a policy moves per step, which bounds how stale a sample can be before correction matters.
Long-tail rollouts Over-provision prompts, stop at the target count, and recycle the unfinished ones. Partial rollout: pause generation, park the trajectory with its tokens and log-probs, resume under newer weights. Some stacks mask the stale prefix out of the loss; PipelineRL instead keeps the stale KV cache and lets sequences span versions. Central. A slow oracle is the long tail. The stall is already the thing these mechanisms exist to absorb.
Train/inference mismatch Store the behavior log-probs per token at sampling time, then correct in the loss by reweighting, masking or rejecting. Replay the MoE routing and the sampler's truncation mask so both policies share an action support. Or engineer the two paths to bitwise agreement and gate it in CI. Worse for non-text vocabularies and custom tokenizers, which is the normal case for sequence or molecular representations.
Staleness admission A capacity formula bounding accepted-plus-running samples against a maximum-staleness budget, then masking or dropping what exceeds it. One real bug found in the code: resuming from a checkpoint without resetting the accepted counter makes staleness unbounded. Central, and the place to be careful. Staleness is bounded at submission, not at reward arrival, so a slow oracle holds a slot without shrinking the budget.
Environment fleets MicroVM or container sandboxes at very large scale, with pause while the policy is thinking (which can be most of a sandbox's lifetime), fork to grade a copy without side effects, and periodic snapshot. Plus memory overcommit, overlay images, and ordered trajectory logs. Fork-to-grade maps directly onto expensive simulators: branch from an identical state so group comparisons are not confounded by where each rollout started.

Three traps found by reading, not by using

Timeout defaults assume cheap verifiers. One stack's async reward wrapper defaults to a 15-second timeout with a process pool sized for math checkers. Nothing ships defaults for hour-scale rewards.

Dropping stale episodes wastes the expensive thing. prime-rl aggressively drops rollouts past the staleness bound, including in-flight ones. That is safe for a cheap verifier and expensive when each reward cost a GPU-hour. The decoupled-PPO route — never discard, just mask extreme-ratio tokens — fits better.

Loss normalization differs between frameworks. One keeps the pre-masking token count as denominator; another normalizes each loss component by its own global count; a third uses global active-token normalization. Porting a reward function between them silently changes your effective learning rate.

Two things already exist for science

NeMo Gym ships chemistry and assay-ranking environments: a reasoning-chemistry server with formula, reaction and solubility verifiers behind an external oracle service, plus a CRISPR-screen ranking task whose reward is a ranked-relevance metric imported from the benchmark authors' own package. And Biomni-R0, the one RL-trained biomedical agent found in the survey, was trained on SkyRL using async rollout scheduling specifically to hide slow tool latency. Both are proof the plumbing works; neither is yet pointed at an expensive physical oracle.

06 — Deep research agents

Long-horizon agent RL

The mechanics that make a 23-step, 200-page research rollout trainable, and the failure modes that only appear in the code.

The mechanics that matter

  • Observation masking. Retrieved text and tool output must be excluded from the loss. This is the single most common bug: one widely-copied open implementation creates the loss mask after computing advantages, so the advantage estimator still sees retrieved text. Another re-tokenizes the trajectory string, which breaks token alignment entirely. Token-in-token-out plumbing exists in the production stacks precisely to avoid this.
  • Credit assignment across turns. The common baseline broadcasts one trajectory-level advantage to every turn. The 2026 literature is a series of attempts to do better — turn-level rewards, information-gain shaping, chunked ratios where a chunk ends at a tool call.
  • Context management as a learned behavior. Kimi-Researcher reports the model learning to retain and drop documents, extending single rollouts past fifty iterations; the ablation says the context-managed model used about 30% more iterations. Most open implementations instead hand-write a summarization pipeline.
  • Reward for open-ended answers. Rubric rewards with an evolving per-prompt rubric buffer, plus citation and format terms. One composite in a recent open system: rubric 0.5, citation 0.2, format 0.2, search-turn count 0.1.
  • Simulated environments. Several systems train against an offline snapshot of the web before moving to the live one, and report matching reward curves. One trains a simulated search engine by telling it the gold answer and controlling noise on an exponential curriculum.

Where the science agents stand

The headline scientific agent systems do no RL. The large closed-loop campaigns run entirely in context, with no weight updates, and the long-horizon literature agents are prompted multi-agent pipelines. Their published failure modes — chasing statistically significant but irrelevant findings, and hallucinating entities that do not exist — are exactly what training against verified outcomes would target. That is the clearest unexploited opening in the whole survey.

Two findings recur and should set expectations. First, RL here sharpens more than it discovers: a systematic study across several design tasks found pass@k gains with reduced diversity and novelty, capped by reward accuracy and task headroom. Second, diversity collapse is the default: one system needed an embedding-level diversity penalty because sequence-level penalties destabilized training outright.

08 — Openings

Where the transfer is real

Ordered by how much of the work is already done for you. Each one names the frontier mechanism it borrows and the thing that has to be built.

01

Treat an expensive oracle as a straggler, not as a blocker

The whole async apparatus — partial rollout, version-stamped samples, staleness admission, importance correction — exists to absorb rollouts that take far longer than the rest of their batch. A docking run, a folding call, a short simulation or a plate of assays is structurally identical to a 200-turn agent trajectory. The change needed is mostly in the budgets: raise the staleness bound past the oracle's latency, mask rather than drop what exceeds it, and size admission control in oracle slots rather than in tokens.

borrows: partial rollout + decoupled-PPO masking + staleness admission · builds: oracle-slot admission control, a durable job queue, a content-addressed result cache

02

A multi-fidelity reward cascade with an audit tier

Score every rollout with something cheap, promote only the top of each group to the expensive oracle, and hold a third oracle entirely out of the reward as an audit. This is already how the tiered rewards in frontier environments are built — GLM-5's three-level rendering reward, K3's correctness gate before its performance score — and NeMo Gym's recompute-rewards-from-stored-rollouts path means the promotion step needs no re-inference. The audit tier is what tells you whether your policy is learning the objective or learning the surrogate's blind spots.

borrows: tiered rewards + reward recomputation + held-out orthogonal filters · builds: calibration between tiers, and a promotion rule

03

Correct for the selection bias that a cascade introduces

This is the subtle one, and I have found no prior work on it. A cascade only sends promoted candidates to the expensive oracle, so the rewarded set is not a sample of the policy's distribution. Worse, oracle latency correlates with the property being measured — larger complexes take longer — so a timeout is informative censoring, not missing-at-random. DeepSeek documented the same class of bug when preempted long rollouts biased their data toward brevity, and solved it by resuming rather than discarding. The fix here is an importance weight on the promotion step, pushed through the existing correction hook.

borrows: the length-bias-from-preemption lesson + existing importance-correction hooks · builds: inverse-propensity weights for promotion; censoring-aware group assembly

04

A judge that must name the failure, audited by a meta-judge

DeepSeekMath-V2's verifier does not emit a score; it writes an analysis that cites specific flaws, and a separately trained meta-verifier checks whether the cited flaw actually exists and justifies the score. They report analysis quality rising from 0.85 to 0.96 at equal score accuracy. For biology this is the route to a reward where no deterministic checker exists: a critic that must say why a design or an analysis is wrong, with the citation checkable against structure, data or the record. It also directly attacks the oracle-hallucination problem that makes learned predictors dangerous as rewards.

borrows: generative verifier + meta-verifier + honest-self-critique weighting · builds: a rubric vocabulary of real failure modes, and sparse ground truth to calibrate against

05

Tournament rewards where only comparisons are trustworthy

ArenaRL's starting observation is discriminative collapse: once outputs are good, pointwise reward noise drowns the advantage signal. Their answer is to rank a group by pairwise comparison, with seeded single-elimination giving O(N) comparisons at near round-robin quality. Many biological measurements are only reliably relative — same-plate comparisons, paired competition, relative free-energy differences. Seeding the bracket with a cheap predictor and spending the expensive comparison only on bracket matches ties the oracle budget directly to the advantage signal.

borrows: tournament-ranked group rewards + O(N) seeded brackets · builds: mapping a comparison assay onto a bracket, and tie handling

06

Conjunctive gating instead of a weighted sum of properties

Additive rubric rewards are hackable: the policy buys points on the cheap criteria. The 2026 result is that conjunctive aggregation with a veto fixes it. The matching design already exists in Qwen's math reward — a hard verifier term that guarantees any passing sample outranks any failing one, with the dense score only ordering within each class. For multi-property design that shape is strictly safer than a weighted sum, and NVIDIA's decoupled multi-reward normalization is the complement: normalize each channel separately so no single one dominates.

The cheapest version of the same instinct comes from a proof system that suppresses false positives by taking the minimum score across several verifiers. With several imperfect property predictors, the minimum is the conservative aggregate: a design has to survive all of them rather than average well across them.

borrows: verified-gate-plus-surrogate reward shape + conjunctive aggregation + min-across-verifiers + per-channel normalization · builds: which properties are gates and which are scores

07

Specialists, then on-policy distillation

Biology is the ideal case for this pattern and nobody has applied it. The field is naturally a set of narrow domains with incompatible verifiers — structure, property prediction, sequence analysis, data analysis, protocol code. Train each as its own RL specialist against its own checker, then merge by on-policy distillation with the teacher log-ratio as a dense per-token reward. It avoids the reward interference of a mixed objective, and it is how K3 merged nine experts and how Nemotron-3-Ultra merged four. Frameworks support it today.

borrows: multi-teacher on-policy distillation · builds: the domain split, and a routing rule for which teacher owns which prompt

08

Fork the simulator to grade, so groups start from identical states

Frontier sandboxes support fork and snapshot so a judge can inspect a copy without side effects. Applied to a stochastic simulator this does something a group-relative method needs: every candidate in a group is compared from exactly the same initial state, so the advantage reflects the candidate rather than the seed. Pause-while-thinking is the companion saving, since in agent rollouts the sandbox spends most of its life idle.

borrows: sandbox fork, snapshot and pause · builds: checkpointable simulator wrappers

09

A provenance reward for research agents

For a literature-and-database agent, the cheap verifiable signal is not whether the prose sounds right but whether every cited identifier exists, resolves, and was actually retrieved during the trajectory. That makes a deterministic verifier out of an open-ended task, and it attacks the hallucinated-entity failure that the prompted science agents report. Build the environment on versioned database snapshots with cached tool calls and time-split evaluation, and synthesize the questions by walking the ontology graph — which is exactly how the frontier search agents generate their training questions, and how K3's task synthesizer works.

borrows: knowledge-graph question synthesis + set-theoretic answer verification + observation masking · builds: versioned snapshots, an identifier resolver, retrieval-trace checking

10

Auto-curriculum tied to the solver's own pass rate

Two independent versions of this exist: a generator rewarded on a triangular curve that peaks when the solver passes about half of its attempts, and the pass@8-is-zero-but-pass@512-is-not filter that produced a measurable gain for GLM-4.5. Both solve the problem that most groups have zero advantage. For design campaigns that means automatically selecting targets at the edge of current capability instead of burning oracle budget on the saturated and the impossible.

borrows: triangular difficulty reward + pass-rate curricula + zero-variance filtering · builds: a difficulty estimate that does not itself cost oracle calls

11

Single-rollout critic PPO for rewards that arrive once, late

Group-relative methods need 8–32 oracle evaluations per prompt in lockstep. When you can afford one measurement per proposal and it comes back days later, SAO is the matching objective: no group, a critic supplying the baseline, importance masking against the rollout log-probs, and credit that skips observation tokens. The older diagnosis behind it matters too — a critic trained from scratch on long trajectories collapses unless you pretrain the value function and decouple its discount from the policy's. A value model pretrained on historical campaign data is a cheap dense surrogate.

borrows: SAO + value pretraining + decoupled GAE · builds: the critic's pretraining corpus; nothing public implements SAO

12

Adversarial audits as a standing part of the loop

Every lab that scaled RL reports reward hacking as the default. In biology the oracles are more hackable than a math checker, because a learned predictor can be driven far out of distribution. The pattern to copy is concrete: ship each oracle with a held-out second oracle, a degenerate-output detector, and an explicit exploit list applied as a tie-breaker among already-correct candidates rather than as a competing objective. Treat an exploit the policy finds as a measurement of your environment.

borrows: hack detectors, held-out verifiers, rule-filter-then-judge guards, inoculation findings · builds: an adversarial test set per oracle

09 — Method

How this was put together

Twelve research agents ran in parallel, one per track. Each cloned the relevant repositories and read the loss files, rollout loops, launch scripts and configs, then checked what it found against the papers and model cards — and each was asked to flag, separately, what the code showed that the published description did not. That turned out to be the most valuable column: several widely-cited recipes do not match their open implementations.

Every claim in the underlying notes carries an evidence tag: read directly in code, read as a tool-generated page summary, from a secondary source, or inferred. Paper figures were mostly obtained through a summarizing fetch, so exact numbers are reported as-stated rather than independently re-derived. Several pages would not render, several arXiv fetches were rate-limited, and two search budgets ran out; each track lists what it could not read.
