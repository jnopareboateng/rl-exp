## 2. Question, arms, endpoint, statistics, locking

**Question.** Does online RL add value for held-out structure recovery beyond cheaper alternatives, at matched inference cost?

**Arms at 4B** (each trained on Train only, selected on Val; token cost per answer reported for every arm):

| Arm | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| C0 constant, C1 copy-nearest | CPU controls; copy-nearest copies a Train structure (answer copying, not retrieval-augmented prompting) | none |
| A0 / A0n | Base model, thinking on / off | inference |
| A4 | Base + in-context retrieval: the 3 nearest Train prompts with their reference SMILES in the prompt | inference |
| A2 | Answer-only SFT (LoRA; empty think block, then the SMILES) | small |
| A1 | Trace SFT (LoRA; reference traces cut after the answer line) | small |
| A3 | Rejection-sampling FT: keep self-generated Train rollouts that reach conn_match, SFT on them | sampling + small |
| A5 | Online RL (GRPO-family, init from the best of A0/A1/A2 on Val), answer-only mode first (about 100 tokens per rollout); thinking-mode RL only if compute allows | large |

**Endpoint (one, for every arm and size):** core@1 = conn_match or non-empty Murcko-scaffold match, mean over 8 samples, drug as the unit. Secondary and reported, never primary: exact@1, stereo_exact@1, mean Tanimoto, maj@8 (deployable plurality vote), pass@8 (oracle coverage, an upper bound that needs the answer to select).
**Confirmatory contrast (one):** A5 at its Val-selected checkpoint versus the best non-RL arm by Val, on Test-T, decided by the exact two-sided sign-flip permutation test on per-drug differences at alpha 0.05, with wins, losses and ties shown. Test-R and every other comparison are exploratory, labelled so, with no adjustment claimed.
**Statistics.** The sign-flip p-value decides; cluster-bootstrap intervals are descriptive. One training seed per arm, so seed variance is unmeasured and stated as a limitation. At 41 Test-T drugs only large effects can be detected; no power claim beyond the planning numbers below.
**Power** (planning numbers from Val, not a guarantee). For 41 independent Test-T drugs (41 components, so each drug is a unit), a two-sided test at alpha 0.05 with 80% power on the paired mean difference (normal approximation) detects 2.8 x SD / sqrt(41) = 0.44 x SD, where SD is the standard deviation of the per-drug paired differences (2.8 = 1.96 + 0.84). The first review's planning cases (valid-only Tanimoto, paired SD 0.046 for A2 vs 9B and 0.073 for 27B vs C0) gave 0.021 and 0.033. Recomputed from our own Val estimates (20 drugs, `runs/colab-s1/*/scored.v3.jsonl`, the 10 pairs among the five k=8, T=1.0 arms: untrained 4B, A4, A2, 9B, 27B-FP8; SD with n-1): the paired per-drug differences of **fv_tanimoto**, the endpoint, have SD 0.035 to 0.077 (median 0.065), so a minimum detectable difference of about 0.015 to 0.034; of mean Tanimoto over all attempts, SD 0.023 to 0.057, about 0.010 to 0.025; of lift, SD 0.013 to 0.043, about 0.006 to 0.019 (all 78 pairs among the 13 k=8 runs: fv_tanimoto SD up to 0.095, about 0.041). The contrast that matters is RL against these arms: for the RL-full checkpoints s20, s30 and s40 (T=0.6 and 1.0, scored with `rlexp.metrics` from `runs/colab-s1/rlfull-*/samples.jsonl`) against A2 at the same temperature, A4, 9B and 27B-FP8, the fv_tanimoto SD is 0.040 to 0.083 (median 0.058), about 0.017 to 0.036 (median 0.025) minimum detectable. For scale, the Val fv_tanimoto gaps between those five arms are 0.004 to 0.099 (median 0.039). core@1 and exact@1 are not usable: exact@1 is 0 for every arm, and every positive core@1 score on Val (29 runs, the RL-full ones included) sits in one 4-drug component; with n non-tied units the smallest two-sided exact sign-flip p-value is 2^(1-n), so fewer than six nonzero component differences (five give 0.0625) cannot reach p <= 0.05. Limits: the SDs come from 20 Val drugs (16 components), zero-RL arms and early RL checkpoints, not from the final RL checkpoint on Test-T; one seed per arm; failing to reject is not equivalence.
**Locking.** Reward weights, extractor, decoding, arms and hyperparameters, checkpoint-selection rule, endpoint and contrast are fixed and committed under tag `prereg-v1` before any Test-split sampling. Decisions (G0, G1, reward tuning, model selection) use Train rollouts of the untrained model and Val only. Each frozen arm is scored on Test once; any later change gets a new tag and is reported as a deviation.
**If A5 does not win:** the deliverable is the benchmark, the controls, and the SFT/RFT results (a negative result for RL is a result). 9B and 27B are entered only after 4B A5 wins; the 27B then needs its own matched SFT comparator.
**Revised before the lock (2026-10-17, after the Val baselines of section 5 and an independent review of this plan; the lift metric itself is still to be checked independently before `prereg-v1`).** These supersede the endpoint, contrast and decoding paragraphs above where they differ.
(a) *Endpoint.* core@1 is near 0 for every arm, and Test-T drugs approved in 2025-26 are unlikely to be recalled (inferred: the models' knowledge cutoffs are undocumented, and most of these structures were public years before approval, section 3), so exact and core recovery stay near 0 for every arm; a sign-flip test on them is decided by ties. After a second independent review, the single confirmatory endpoint is **fv_tanimoto**: the mean count-vector Tanimoto of the **first valid, finished answer among up to 8 samples** of each prompt row (0 when none is valid), drug-balanced. It is the deployable "resample until RDKit accepts the SMILES" policy, so arms are compared at matched validity without conditioning away hard cases, and its cost (`fv_samples`, mean generations per answer, retrieval prompts and retries included) is reported beside it. Reported, never the primary: expected Tanimoto over all attempts (invalid = 0), validity, valid-only similarity, core@1, exact@1, stereo-exact, maj@8, pass@8 (oracle) and the **profile-specific lift** (answer-to-own-reference Tanimoto minus its mean to the other drugs' references of the split; `fv_lift` for the first-valid policy). The lift is a diagnostic of association between the drug and the policy's output, not a proof of profile use: a constant answer has lift exactly 0 only if the completion process does not depend on the drug (a fixed molecule scored +0.18 when only one drug finished), and a policy keyed on nuisance cues such as prompt length can score high. Claim language: a positive fv_tanimoto difference is "higher delivered similarity at a stated cost"; the word "profile" requires a positive lift above the nearest-profile control C1 AND a matched shuffled-or-removed-profile comparison that keeps prompt length, section order and format.
(b) *Contrast.* Unchanged in form: A5 at its Val-selected checkpoint against the best non-RL arm by Val on Test-T, cluster-level exact sign-flip on the per-drug fv_tanimoto differences, plus a studentized component-bootstrap line as a sensitivity check; the p-value is not assumption-free (components are the exchangeable units). Test-R is exploratory.
(c) *Decoding.* Every stochastic arm gets the same tuning opportunity: temperature from {0.3, 0.6, 1.0} chosen on Val by fv_tanimoto at k=8, then frozen. Near-greedy decoding (0.01) is reported at k=1 as "near-greedy" and is not selectable (eight near-greedy samples of a prompt are nearly one sample). RL runs use EVAL_K=8 for their Val evaluations from now on (the first pilot used 4), so the in-training Val fv_tanimoto has the endpoint's k. Thinking mode, prompt, top-p, token cap and stopping are frozen; a length-terminated answer is a failure for every arm; the cost report includes retrieval prompts, failed attempts and retries.
(d) *Controls required before any RL claim* (additions to the arm table): the exact checkpoint A5 starts from, with identical inference; **validity-only RL** from the same checkpoint (isolates syntax); **profile-free RL** (the profile replaced by a fixed string, same reward: what the reward gives without the profile); continued SFT with a small learning-rate and step search; dense-reward-ranked RFT from a matched rollout pool (A3 as specified keeps only identity hits and would starve); first-valid and best-of-k selection; the identification and name-given diagnostics with fixed aliases and a profile-plus-name variant; and a frozen shuffled-profile diagnostic (the lift is its dual and needs no extra sampling).
(e) *Order.* The zero-RL size and cost sweep (4B, 9B, 3.5-27B, 3.8-27B) is the main study; RL is a bounded case study. The RL size is chosen by predeclared Train/Val validity, headroom and compute rules, not by a positive 4B RL effect. Prefer repeat training seeds for the final RL and comparator pair over a wide RL size ladder. Cap the number of Val-guided searches.
(f) *Allowed claims.* Positive: improved expected similarity or profile-specific similarity under a fixed budget, against specified tuned controls, on this split and cohort. Negative: a bound for the tested recipe and budget (non-significance is not "no effect"). Not claimed: novel-drug discovery, biological efficacy, universal RL superiority or failure, molecules unseen in pretraining.

## 3. Phase 0: data foundation (CPU)

1. `rlexp.data_prep` on the pooled train+val rows: drop PD (155 rows) and the 11 combination rows; references from ChEMBL cross-checked against the SFT answers, hand-resolved where they disagree; final approval year = manual > audit > ChEMBL/openFDA.
2. Splits (seed 0, built): Test-T = first approved anywhere in 2025-26 (41 drugs, 100 rows); buffer = their component neighbours (19 drugs); Test-R 30 drugs (80 rows) and Val 20 (58 rows) drawn by whole components; Train = rest (241 drugs, 687 rows); 3 excluded. No pair across splits reaches Tanimoto 0.4 or shares a scaffold. Components join drugs with Tanimoto >= 0.4 or an identical non-empty scaffold. The 41 Test-T drugs lie in 41 different components (8 of them also contain excluded buffer drugs), so within Test-T every drug is its own exchangeable unit. Exclusions: unresolved disagreement, no year, unextractable reference.
3. Exposure covariates (PubChem creation year, PubMed count) per drug; Test-T results are also reported by exposure tercile. Approval date is not disclosure date: PubChem records of 40 of the 41 cohort drugs were created 3 to 20 years before approval (median 10.5), so Test-T is a temporal cohort, not a set of unseen molecules, and most of its structures were public years before approval.
4. Data manifest (sha256 of raw and processed files) recorded in every run.

## 4. Phase 1: reward v2 and its tests (CPU)

Gate: `</think>` closed, a SMILES extracted and parseable, else 0. Reference and answer standardised identically.
`r = 0.05 + 0.6 * Tanimoto + 0.3 * [same non-empty Murcko scaffold]` with Morgan count-vector Tanimoto, `r = 1.0` when the answer's identity key (InChIKey first block) equals the reference's or a curated alternate's (5 references have one); stereo reported only. Answers over 1,500 characters or 400 atoms, or with dummy atoms, are invalid. Weights are provisional and tuned only on Train rollouts and Val.
Offline tests before any GPU RL (Train and Val only; measured: T1 745/745, T2 mean 0.136, T3 200/200, constant-answer bar on the reward scale 0.160 on Train and 0.147 on Val: glossary in section 5): T1 reference completion scores 1.0 on its own row for 98% or more (misses listed); T2 another drug's reference averages 0.2 or less; T3 randomised SMILES of the reference score identically;
T4 the 77 answer-format fixtures behave as recorded; T5 on real Train rollouts at least 30% of groups show reward std of 0.05 or more **among valid completions only**. The extractor's 11 known silent-wrong cases are measured on real rollouts:
if more than 1% of outputs differ between this extractor and "last SMILES-only line", switch to the latter before `prereg-v1`. Codex reviews `reward.py` before use.
**Measured on Val samples** (2026-10-17; `scripts/offline_audit.py` with `results/extractor_audit.json` and `results/t5.json`; Val only, because the one set of Train rollouts, 96 prompts under the dataset prompt, is almost all cut at the cap; 29 runs: 23 zero-RL, SFT, retrieval and name-given runs with 7,520 distinct samples, and 6 RL-full checkpoint runs, `rlfull-s20`, `-s30` and `-s40` at T=0.6 and 1.0, with 2,784). *T5 fails for every arm:* the share of prompt rows whose reward std over valid finished samples is at least 0.05 is at most 15.5% in the 17 non-RL runs with k >= 4 (A2 at T=0.6; 9B told the name) against the 30% bar, 5.2% (3 of 58 rows) for A2 at T=1.0, the RL sampling temperature, and 0% to 5.2% for the RL-full checkpoints. Over all 8 samples with invalid ones at reward 0 (the spread an RL update sees) 91% of A2 rows at T=1.0 reach 0.05, against 31%, 17% and 5% for the RL-full checkpoints s20, s30 and s40 at T=1.0, so the within-group signal is mostly validity, not graded similarity, and it shrinks as RL proceeds (inferred). *Answer reader:* 227 of the 10,304 samples (2.2%; 2.2% of the 9,057 finished) differ between this extractor and the last SMILES-only line, so the rule above says switch. All 227 are in the 23 non-RL runs (3.0% of their 7,520 samples, 3.2% of the 6,275 finished); the RL-full checkpoints have none. Of the 227, 197 are samples with no line that is a SMILES by itself, where this extractor reads a SMILES-like token from a line with other words or trailing punctuation (97% of these molecules have 8 or more heavy atoms, the size of the smallest reference); 26 are tiny answers (under 8 heavy atoms) that the stricter rule reads and this one refuses by its 8-character guard; 4 (0.05% of the 7,520) are samples where the two return different molecules. By arm: A2 3 of 464 (0.6%) at T=1.0 and none at T=0.3 or 0.6; untrained 4B 6.7% to 9.3%; A1 13%; A4 9.7%; 9B 0% to 1.5%; 27B-FP8 1.5%. The choice (switch as written, or amend the rule to count only different molecules) is a freeze-checklist item in `docs/prereg-v1.md` section 8.

**Class-information ceiling** (`scripts/class_ceiling.py`, ChEMBL_37, `results/class_ceiling.json`, 1,786 drugs in 771 components): two retrieval rules (1-nearest-neighbour, 5-medoid) over coarsened class facts (ATC, mechanism, target, indication) predict a drug's structure from its neighbours. With neighbours allowed by the pairwise split rule (no Tanimoto >= 0.4, no shared scaffold) the 1-NN scores 0.201 against 0.169 for the best constant (+0.032, component-bootstrap [+0.015, +0.051]; Train targets +0.001, compatible with gains up to +0.014). An independent review showed the real split separates whole connected components; with the target's whole component excluded the 1-NN scores 0.163 (-0.006 [-0.011, +0.001] against the best constant, compatible with gains up to +0.001; Train targets -0.014 [-0.024, -0.004]) and beats only a Train-fixed constant over the whole pool (+0.017), not for Train targets (-0.011). Reading: class facts through these two rules do not beat a constant once neighbours respect the split. This does not bound learned predictors, richer facts or free-text profiles (the nearest-profile copy control C1 has profile-specific lift +0.022 at constant-level Tanimoto), and failing to reject zero is not equivalence. Decision: scaling the number of training drugs with synthetic profiles is deprioritised, not ruled out.
Near-greedy decoding (T=0.01, k=1) moves the numbers a lot: A2 valid 0.75, mean reward 0.114 (0.077 at T=1.0); 9B untrained valid 0.60, reward 0.093; but 10% to 24% of near-greedy samples run to the cap (6 of 58 for A2, 13 of 58 for 9B, 14 of 58 for 4B; `results/val_baselines.json`, `runs/colab-s1/g-*`).
A2 training (12 minutes on the A100): teacher-forced Val loss 1.25 after epoch 1 and 1.61, 1.72 after epochs 2 and 3 while train loss fell 0.99, 0.38, 0.14, so one epoch is the best checkpoint and more epochs only memorise the 241 Train drugs. The settings of the A2 and A1 runs behind the Val results above are in `gpu/SFT.md` (Runs made).
Reading (measured, then inferred): (1) no arm clears the constant-answer bar on mean reward, and the 27B's valid answers average 0.18 Tanimoto, the constant's level, so there is no evidence yet that the profile informs the structure beyond a class prior;
(2) naming the drug does not help at 4B and 9B, so SMILES recall, not identification, is the first bottleneck at these sizes (the identification diagnostic, `rlexp.ident`, splits this properly);
(3) core@1 is 0 to 5% for every arm, so with 41 Test-T drugs core@1 can only separate arms with a very large effect; section 2 (a) therefore makes fv_tanimoto the confirmatory endpoint.

## 6. Phase 3: RL pilot (A5)

**Implementation (decided and built 2026-10-17; supersedes the TRL/ms-swift plan; details and gates in `gpu/RL.md`).** One process on the A100: rollouts come from Hugging Face `generate` on the PEFT model being trained (no vLLM, no TRL), with an fp32 LM head in generation and in training, so there is no engine or weight-sync mismatch and the environment is the SFT one. Answer-only completions are short, so training dominates the step time.
Loss: one update per rollout batch, so the importance ratio is 1 and the update is REINFORCE with a group baseline (CISPO's clipped ratio would need old log-probs; documented in code); advantages centred per group, divided by the batch std of the retained centred advantages; zero-variance groups dropped; every token of completion i weighs `A_i / (T_g * G)` (prompt-level aggregation); no KL. A length-terminated answer is a failure (reward 0) and stays in its group; it is not masked, because with a 256-token cap it is a failure to stop, not a long chain of thought. Cap 256 tokens, group 8, temperature 1.0, AdamW without weight decay, LoRA rank 32 on all projections including the GatedDeltaNet ones, learning rate 1e-5 to 3e-5 (at most three settings, on Val), warmup 5 steps, clip 1.0.
Why not TRL: its prompt-level aggregation, batch normalisation of centred advantages, zero-variance dropping and fp32 tied head each needed a hook (`gpu/STACK.md`); a 1,000-line own loop with CPU-tested maths and a tiny-model run is simpler to audit than five hooks. TRL stays the cross-check for thinking-mode RL later.
Measured gates on the A100 (Qwen3.5-4B, A2 adapter): sampling versus training-forward log-probs, mean |diff| 0.015 to 0.018 per token, mean importance ratio 0.994 to 1.000, 8 to 11% of tokens above 0.05, the same with a prompt alone as in a heavily left-padded batch (so padding adds no error; the gap is bf16 decode-versus-prefill noise; the provisional "5% above 0.05" threshold was too strict); smoke gate: 3 real steps, the first update moves the adapter (delta norm 0.016), losses finite, resume reproduces the schedule, seeds and even the rollouts bit for bit in the one smoke run on the A100 (nondeterminism may break this in general). Step cost at 8 prompts: generation 25 s (latency-bound: 256 decode steps), update 10 s.
Batch construction: each update needs at least 16 active drugs after filtering; oversample up to 2x to refill; skip the update and log if fewer than 8 remain.
Gates before real steps: exact template match, step-0 trainer-vs-vLLM log-prob gap, then **parity after a nonzero optimizer update** (a no-op LoRA init passes step 0 trivially), adapter coverage of every projection, five finite steps, peak memory at the 8k cap, kill-and-resume with optimizer and RNG state.
Per step log: reward mean/std, zero-variance share, valid, closed, truncation, length mean and p90, clip, grad norm, and the **collapse score**: distinct modal answers (by connectivity) over distinct drugs in the batch; healthy is near 1, alarm below 0.7.
Val eval every 10 steps (k=8 from now on, the endpoint's k; the first pilot used 4). Stop: Val flat for 3 evals while train reward rises (memorisation); collapse alarm; truncation above 30%. **G2:** Val gain beyond the paired interval, no collapse, resume works.
